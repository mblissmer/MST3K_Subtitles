Subtitles Project

This is a repository for subtitle files for all "classic" Mystery Science Theater 3000 episodes (Seasons 1-10) in SRT format.

Download Complete Classic MST3K Subtitle Archive (ZIP)

"How do I use these files?"

Download the zip file, unzip it, and place the subtitle files in the same folder as your video files.

They must have exactly the same filename as your video file (except for the file extensions of course). If your episode is named "S01E01-TheCrawlingEye-LOL.mp4" then the subtitle file must be named "S01E01-TheCrawlingEye-LOL.srt"

Also we are not responsible if your weird cut of the episode has commercials in it (that's neat, save that!) or other unexpected playback gaps.

Also also, there may be timing issues, where your version might be off from the subtitles by a couple seconds. That's up to you to figure out how to offset the timing for the subtitle. As long as it's consistently x seconds off, and isn't drifting futher and futher off as the episode goes on, that's outside the scope of this project.

FAQ

Why do this?

Sometimes it's hard to tell what people are saying. Look, us MSTIEs aren't getting any younger, okay?!

Also, subtitles for this show in particular are in a pretty rough shape.

  • Most have decent subtitles downloadable from the Gizmoplex (RIP).
  • Some episodes have solid subtitles on their DVD release.
  • A few stragglers have subtitles of nebulous origin from (scary voice) the internet.
  • Some of those internet downloads are made from some bad ripped-from-tv verison of the episode that includes a commercial for a thorough but gentle laxative.
  • Some don't have any at all, in any location, anywhere on the internet (that we could find).

Why "Good Enough (tm)" instead of "Absolutely Perfect?" Isn't our favorite TV show worth PERFECTION??

Subtitling MST3K episodes offers a pretty unique challenge:

  • Riffers frequently talk over movie dialog
  • Sometimes it's not clear who said what
  • Movies with bad audio tracks can have completely unintelligible lines
  • Episodes are 90 minutes long. With 197 episodes, that's nearly 300 hours of dialog to get down.
  • This is being done in our free time for no money and we're old and have kids.

Isn't there another captions project already?

There is another effort to "burn-in" subtitles (captions), located here.

This is a really great project with really high ambitions: They have color-coded subtitles, coordinated with who is speaking, as well as animated subs during the host segment transitions. The captions are also "burned in" to each episode, meaning they're part of the video and always on screen. This requires uploading a full episode onto YouTube or another platform. Their target is 100% accuracy. It's really extremely impressive.

The downside that flows naturally from this level of ambition is that it's very labor-intensive and therefore a slow, painstaking process to finish each episode.

How is this project different?

Here, we're going for a lower-effort, maximum-effect angle. We're not aiming for 100% accuracy, hence the title of the project.

If we can get all 197 classic episodes here and accurate to like 70%, that would be HUGE, and also... importantly... Good Enough (tm). After that, if we're so inclined, we can go back and make improvements!

Why SRT files instead of a format with more features?

The downside of SRT subtitle files is that they have basically zero features. No colored text, no alternate fonts, no screen positioning, no animation... it's basically just a text file with a bunch of timestamps in it.

The upside, however, is near-universal compatibility with all video playback programs/apps. If it supports subtitles at all, it supports SRT. Which sounds.... Good Enough (tm)
